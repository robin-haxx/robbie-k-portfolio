Current focus

Avian Age

A framework for digital media about the natural history of Aotearoa. Grounded in real ecology research, as well as emergent systems design principles, it features terrestrial flora and avifauna, climate and landscape.
I adapt this into games and installations; that teach how the native ecosystem holds together, through hands-on play.

The game · In development

Avian Age

A real-time ecosystem game set in glacial-age Aotearoa. You play a kaitiaki, a guardian, keeping moa alive on the mountain tops: growing the plants they feed on, and calling storms to hide them from the pouākai, Haast's eagle.

Moa, eagles, kea and forest birds each live by their own rules in a running ecosystem. You have a few tools, and you learn the rest by watching what the land does through the seasons.

Museum installation · Te Manawa, Palmerston North

A River Older Than Mountains

An adaptation of the Avian Age framework for Te Manawa Museum. Visitors follow the Manawatū through two million years on a wall-sized living map, driven from a touchscreen beside it: a sea strait closes, a young river crosses the low land, and the Ruahine and Tararua ranges rise around a river that holds its course.

Ice ages turn forest to tussock and back, four great eruptions reset the land, and thirteen North Island birds, from giant moa to huia, live through all of it. Visitors keep the ecosystem healthy by choosing the right plants for the climate.
